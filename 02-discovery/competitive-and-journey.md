# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** tech enthusiast
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** to spread the cost of big purchases without being blocked mid-checkout.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** BNPL limit constraints. The basket exceeds their available limit, so they split the purchase, abandon it, or use a different credit product.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** a. Split. They break the basket into two orders, putting what fits on Riverty and the rest on Klarna, PayPal Pay Later or debit.
b. Switch. They put the whole basket on a revolving credit card or a competitor’s instalment product.
c. Abandon. They leave, wait for repayments to free up the limit, or buy elsewhere.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** They open the Riverty app, check their open balance, and work out the gap themselves.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** for the customer, the limit shows up at the worst moment, mid-checkout. They then spend time on a second provider’s onboarding and identity check, and end up with split obligations.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** research shows that even two schedules inside Riverty cause confusion (T-103, T-104), so three providers is worse. Route (b) may also mean paying interest, which some users actively avoid (N-05).
- **Your journey map file name (e.g. journey-map.html):** riverty_card_future_state_journey
